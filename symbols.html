Circuit symbols

The symbols used in the lessons and diagrams. Some textbooks draw a few symbols differently (for example a resistor as a box), so always check the legend of the drawing you are reading.

Resistor

Opposes current. Unit: ohm (Ω)

Capacitor

Stores energy in an electric field. Unit: farad (F)

Inductor

Stores energy in a magnetic field. Unit: henry (H)

DC cell or battery

Long plate is positive. Unit: volt (V)

AC source

Sinusoidal supply

Switch (open)

Opens or closes a circuit

Lamp

Converts electrical energy to light

Fuse

Melts to open the circuit on overcurrent

Diode

Conducts in one direction

LED

Light-emitting diode

Ammeter

Measures current; connect in series

Voltmeter

Measures voltage; connect in parallel

Motor

Converts electrical to mechanical energy

Generator

Converts mechanical to electrical energy

NO contact

Normally open contact

NC contact

Normally closed contact

Relay or contactor coil

Operates contacts when energised

Circuit breaker (single-line)

Interrupts fault current in a single-line diagram

Isolator (single-line)

Provides an isolating gap, normally off load

Voltage transformer

Steps voltage down for meters and relays

Current transformer

Ring on a conductor; steps current down

Earth (ground)

Connection to earth
